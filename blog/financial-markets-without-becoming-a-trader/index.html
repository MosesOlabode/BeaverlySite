Markets for non-traders

Can I participate in financial markets without becoming a trader?

Yes. You can delegate some or most of the market work. The important question is what kind of delegation you want — and what you still want to control.

September 12, 20266 min read

A lot of financial-market software assumes you already want to be a trader.

It gives you charts, indicators, scanners, signals, order tickets and settings. Those can be useful tools. They still leave you with the job of watching the market, deciding what matters and keeping the process running.

If that is not the job you want, there are other ways to approach it.

You first need to decide what you want to delegate

“I do not want to trade manually” can mean several different things.

  • You do not want to watch charts all day.
  • You do not want to make every individual market decision yourself.
  • You do not want to build or maintain a trading system.
  • You want your money to remain in an account you control.
  • You still want to understand what the software is doing.

Those preferences lead to different products.

Signals reduce research, not the job

A signal service tells you what someone or something thinks you should consider doing. You usually still have to decide whether to follow it, enter the action yourself, manage it and keep checking what happens next.

Signals can fit people who want help finding opportunities while keeping each decision manual.

Copy trading delegates decisions to another trader

Copy trading can reduce manual work further. Your account follows another trader or portfolio according to the provider's rules.

That can fit someone who explicitly wants to mirror another person's activity. The central question becomes who you are copying and how that relationship behaves when conditions change.

A fixed bot delegates a set of rules

A traditional bot can be a good fit when you already know exactly what rules you want automated.

The tradeoff is that somebody still has to choose, configure or maintain those rules. For experienced traders, that may be exactly what they want. For someone trying to leave trading complexity behind, it can feel like becoming the operator of another trading system.

Goal-led automation starts somewhere else

Another approach is to start with what you are trying to achieve rather than with the mechanics of a trading setup.

That is the experience Chilla is built around.

You tell Chilla what you are working towards. Chilla handles the product complexity, narrows what is actually available, and brings you the small material choices that still belong to you. Once those choices are settled, you can review the PLAN before anything goes live.

Once you activate that work, Chilla can carry it within those boundaries and report back.

Do I still need to understand anything?

Yes — but you should not need a trading education just to operate the product.

You should understand the choices that materially affect the work you authorize. You should know where your money stays. You should know what the software can do without asking again. And you should know how to pause, stop or change the work.

The product should carry the rest of the machinery.

Can I keep my own broker or provider account?

With non-custodial automation, yes, when your account is supported.

Chilla works through supported external accounts. Beaverly does not hold the funds in those accounts. Connecting an account also does not automatically authorize live work; that decision remains separate.

Does automation mean constant activity?

No.

Software that is supposed to do the work should also be able to stay out when there is nothing appropriate to do within the active task. More activity is not automatically better work.

Chilla can report back when it acted and when it stayed out. Flow remains available when you want to inspect the activity trail.

Who is this kind of product for?

Goal-led automation can make sense if you are more interested in the outcome and boundaries than in operating a trading workflow yourself.

Manual trading may still fit you better if you enjoy making each decision. A fixed bot may fit better if you want to design the rules. Copy trading may fit better if you specifically want to follow another trader.

Chilla is for a different job: carry the market work while you keep control of the important boundaries.
