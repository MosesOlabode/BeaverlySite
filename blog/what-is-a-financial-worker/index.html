What do we mean by a financial worker?

Software you can talk to, give work to, and hear back from — without turning yourself into the operator of all the machinery underneath.

September 13, 20264 min read

“Financial worker” can mean different things in different contexts. At Beaverly, we use it in a specific product sense.

A financial worker is software you can talk to that can carry supported financial work within the boundaries you approve and report back.

Chilla is Beaverly’s financial worker. It begins with supported work in the financial markets.

Why not just call it a chatbot?

A chatbot can answer a question and stop there. Chilla is meant to carry ongoing work after the conversation moves on.

You can come back later and ask what happened. Chilla reports back, and Flow is available when you want the activity trail.

Is a financial worker the same as a copilot?

Not quite.

A copilot usually helps you do the work. You remain the person operating the process. A worker relationship goes further: software can carry supported ongoing work while you stay involved where your decision actually matters.

Is it another trading bot?

That is not the relationship Chilla is built around.

Traditional trading automation often starts with a strategy, rules, code or a bot the user selects and operates. Chilla starts with what you are working towards. It handles more of the product complexity, brings the material choices that remain yours into the conversation, then carries the supported work within what you approve.

Is Chilla a robo-advisor?

No. Beaverly does not position Chilla as a personalized investment adviser or robo-advisor.

Starting from your goal does not silently give Chilla authority to decide which material financial choice is suitable for your personal circumstances. When a material choice still belongs to you, you make it.

Does “worker” mean unlimited control?

No. Delegation and unlimited authority are different things.

Chilla works inside the boundaries you approve. Connecting a trading account does not by itself give Chilla unlimited authority over that account, and material changes that genuinely need you come back to you.

Where does the money stay?

For live work, funds stay with the provider hosting the supported trading account. Beaverly does not hold those market funds.

You can also use Chilla Demo, where real market prices sit underneath a simulated, non-withdrawable balance and simulated execution.

Why call it a worker at all?

Because the product goal is different from giving people more software to operate.

The person should be able to express what they are working towards, keep control of what matters, step away from repetitive monitoring, and come back to a useful report.

That is the relationship Beaverly is building toward when we say financial worker.

The short version

Chilla is a financial worker: tell it what you are working towards, stay in control of the material things that matter, then let Chilla carry supported financial-market work within those boundaries and report back.

You Chill. Chilla Works.
