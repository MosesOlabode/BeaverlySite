1. What Beaverly is — and what it is not

Beaverly Innovative Systems Ltd. provides software that includes Chilla, a user-facing financial worker, and M-II, the financial execution system underneath authorized live work.

Beaverly is not a brokerage, bank, custodian, licensed investment adviser, or portfolio manager. Beaverly does not receive your deposits, make withdrawals from your provider account, transfer your account funds, or hold or custody the money in that account. Live financial activity occurs through a third-party financial account that you choose and authorize.

Chilla is designed around user-selected and user-authorized boundaries. Beaverly does not provide personalized investment recommendations and does not use your personal financial goal or circumstances as authority to choose any material financial configuration for you.

2. Market risk and possible financial loss

Participation in financial markets can result in partial or total loss of capital. Prices can move sharply, liquidity can change, spreads can widen, leverage can amplify gains and losses, and market conditions can behave differently from historical periods.

You should consider your financial circumstances, tolerance for loss, and ability to bear losses before authorizing live work. Beaverly does not determine whether market participation is appropriate for you.

3. No personalized investment recommendation or guaranteed outcome

Beaverly does not provide personalized investment recommendations and does not use your personal goal or circumstances as authority to recommend or choose a particular financial instrument, style, market, account, comfort setting, or allocation for you.

Supported styles, markets, product explanations, generated conversation, Flow reports, demo results, and historical activity are product information, not promises of profit or future performance. No representation is made that any user will achieve returns, avoid losses, or reproduce past activity.

Chilla can simplify the work without becoming the source of the financial choice. It may remove options that are technically unavailable, explain the remaining options, and prepare your choices for review. You decide what material configuration becomes authorized.

4. Provider authorization and bounded live work

Connecting an account and authorizing a Chilla task are separate actions. You first authorize the account connection through the provider's own authorization flow. That provider permission allows Chilla to interact with the account within the permissions you grant, but it does not by itself activate a Chilla task.

Live work begins only after you separately authorize a specific material configuration presented by Chilla. That configuration can include the selected account, predefined style, markets, risk or other boundaries, and related task settings.

Once active, the material configuration is fixed for that specific task. The task may monitor market conditions and take supported market actions without asking you to approve every individual action. The timing and size of an individual action may be determined by the selected predefined style, market conditions, canonical sizing or risk controls, account-provider rules, and other product safeguards.

Chilla does not silently switch the authorized style, market set, account, risk or other material financial setting because a model or system considers another configuration preferable. A material change requires a new validated revision and fresh approval before it replaces the authorized configuration.

You remain responsible for deciding whether to authorize, pause, stop, revise, or discontinue live work and how much capital you make available in the connected account. You may also revoke or disconnect provider access using the controls made available by Beaverly or the provider, as applicable.

5. Technology and execution risks

Automated financial systems depend on software, networks, market data, providers, and infrastructure. Risks include:

  • network latency, disconnection, outages, or delayed provider acknowledgements;
  • stale, delayed, malformed, or unavailable market data;
  • provider restrictions, maintenance, outages, rejected orders, pricing differences, slippage, or execution delays;
  • software defects, hardware failures, dependency failures, recovery delays, or unexpected interactions between systems;
  • rapid or unusual market conditions that make expected behavior difficult or impossible.

Beaverly designs the Services to fail safely where possible, but does not promise uninterrupted operation, error-free execution, or perfect third-party data.

6. Comfort, safeguards, and limits

Chilla may enforce product safeguards such as comfort boundaries, account/provider minimums, authorization checks, or other controls intended to constrain how authorized work can operate.

These controls are technical safeguards. They do not guarantee that a loss will stay below a particular amount in every market or provider condition. Price gaps, slippage, provider behavior, connectivity issues, or exceptional market movement can affect realized outcomes.

Where a control prevents activity, that does not mean a market outcome would otherwise have been profitable or unprofitable.

Compatibility can vary by region, account type, provider environment, market availability, legal requirements, and third-party policy. A provider can change or withdraw access independently of Beaverly.

Beaverly is not your brokerage, bank, custodian, or financial account provider. Beaverly does not receive deposits into, make withdrawals from, transfer, or custody money in your connected financial account. Funds remain with the account provider you selected.

Demo activity can differ materially from live activity because live accounts introduce provider execution, slippage, liquidity, account rules, connectivity, and other real-world conditions.

Good or poor results in Chilla Demo are not a prediction of future live results.
