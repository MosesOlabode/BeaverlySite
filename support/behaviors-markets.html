Styles and Markets

How to read style cards and temperament review

Simple meanings for the labels you see when choosing a style, reviewing its activity history, and assigning markets.

Where to find this

  • Open Tasks, then start or edit a task.
  • Use the style catalog to choose one style for that task.
  • Use the markets step to choose where that style can run for your connected account.
  • Open View Activity History when you want a deeper activity review before deciding.

How to read a style card

A style card is a short profile for one way Chilla can run. It helps you understand what the style watches for, how it usually acts, and whether its rhythm may feel comfortable for you.

  • Style name: the name of the routine you are selecting.
  • Summary: a quick explanation of what the style is built to watch for.
  • User count: how many users currently have this style in use when that number is available. Treat this as usage context, not a reason to copy other users.
  • Style Description: the main explanation of what the style looks for before it can act.
  • How It Acts: the style’s routine after you select it, such as whether it tends to wait, react quickly, step aside, or follow a more patient sequence.
  • Activity Pattern or Typical Activity: how busy or quiet the style usually was in its study history. This helps you know whether to expect something usually quiet, occasional, or more active.
  • Holding Style: how long the style usually stayed involved after creating an action in its study history. This may be short, longer, or mixed depending on the style and market context.
  • Typical movement level: the style’s account-pressure temperament. A softer ACR means the style historically created smaller temporary account swings. A sharper ACR means it historically allowed larger or faster temporary swings before the routine completed or stepped aside. ACR is about comfort during movement, not profit potential or a safety score.
  • Compatible Markets: the markets where the style can currently run with your selected connected account and trading account path.
  • Activity History or View Activity History: a deeper look at study history, including rhythm, quiet periods, reaction style, account-pressure character, and market-by-market style.

How to read temperament review

Temperament review explains how one style looked in study history. Read it as pattern context, not as a promise of what will happen next.

  • Market tabs or Market name: each tab separates the same style by market because one style can feel calm in one market, busier in another, or use different timing depending on the market.
  • Historical review or Period: the study window being summarized.
  • Consistency Score: how cleanly the style held up in that market during the study window. A higher score means the pattern more often completed cleanly with less account pressure along the way. A lower score means the pattern was more uneven, created more pressure, or had more interruptions. It helps compare historical smoothness across markets, but it is not a promise or a simple win-rate number.
  • Activity Visual: a quick picture of the style’s historical activity shape. It helps you see rhythm at a glance, including quiet stretches, active stretches, or uneven bursts. It is not a profit chart.
  • Action Frequency: how often the style created actions during the study window. This helps you know whether it is usually quiet, occasional, or busy.
  • Reaction Style: how quickly or patiently the style tended to respond after its conditions lined up.
  • Move Personality: the kind of movement the style tended to trade in, such as calmer, balanced, bold, or aggressive movement.
  • Noise Tolerance: how much messy or unclear movement the style historically tolerated before stepping aside. Lower noise tolerance usually means it waits for cleaner conditions. Higher noise tolerance means it can remain active through messier movement.
  • Longest Quiet Period: the longest stretch where the style created no action. Silence can be normal when the style’s rules are not aligned.
  • Allowed Window Adherence: how often the style stayed inside the time or window rules used in the review. This is about discipline and boundaries.
  • Typical Scenarios: examples of situations where the style historically acted or stepped aside. These should help you understand context, not give advice.
  • Style Timeline: a period-by-period view of when activity was higher or lower. It gives more detail than the Activity Visual and helps show whether activity was spread out or clustered.
  • Important Disclaimers: short boundaries to keep in mind when reading study-history information.

How to compare styles

  • Use descriptions and study-history labels to understand fit with your comfort level.
  • Pay attention to account pressure, quiet periods, movement sensitivity, and market differences.
  • Confirm connected account scope and compatible markets before activation.
  • Use Flow after activation to review the actions Chilla records for your task.
