Behaviours and Markets

How to read behaviour cards and temperament review

Simple meanings for the labels you see when choosing a behaviour, reviewing its pattern history, and assigning markets.

Where to find this

  • Open Instructions, then start or edit an instruction.
  • Use the behaviour catalog to choose one behaviour for that instruction.
  • Use the markets step to choose where that behaviour can run for your connected account.
  • Open View Pattern History when you want a deeper temperament review before deciding.

How to read a behaviour card

A behaviour card is a short profile for one Chilla routine. It helps you understand what the behaviour watches for, how it usually acts, and whether its rhythm may feel comfortable for you.

  • Behaviour name: the name of the routine you are selecting.
  • Summary: a quick explanation of what the behaviour is built to watch for.
  • User count: how many users currently have this behaviour in use when that number is available. Treat this as usage context, not a reason to copy other users.
  • Behaviour Description: the main explanation of what the behaviour looks for before it can act.
  • How It Acts: the behaviour’s routine after you select it, such as whether it tends to wait, react quickly, step aside, or follow a more patient sequence.
  • Activity Pattern or Typical Activity: how busy or quiet the behaviour usually was in its study history. This helps you know whether to expect something usually quiet, occasional, or more active.
  • Holding Style: how long the behaviour usually stayed involved after creating an action in its study history. This may be short, longer, or mixed depending on the behaviour and market context.
  • Average Calmness Range (ACR): the behaviour’s account-pressure temperament. A softer ACR means the behaviour historically created smaller temporary account swings. A sharper ACR means it historically allowed larger or faster temporary swings before the routine completed or stepped aside. ACR is about comfort during movement, not profit potential or a safety score.
  • Compatible Markets: the markets where the behaviour can currently run with your selected connected account and provider path.
  • Temperament Review or View Pattern History: a deeper look at study history, including rhythm, quiet periods, reaction style, account-pressure character, and market-by-market behaviour.

How to read temperament review

Temperament review explains how one behaviour looked in study history. Read it as pattern context, not as a promise of what will happen next.

  • Market tabs or Market name: each tab separates the same behaviour by market because one behaviour can feel calm in one market, busier in another, or use different timing depending on the market.
  • Historical review or Period: the study window being summarized.
  • Consistency Score: how cleanly the behaviour’s pattern held up in that market during the study window. A higher score means the pattern more often completed cleanly with less account pressure along the way. A lower score means the pattern was more uneven, created more pressure, or had more interruptions. It helps compare historical smoothness across markets, but it is not a guarantee or a simple win-rate number.
  • Activity Visual: a quick picture of the behaviour’s historical activity shape. It helps you see rhythm at a glance, including quiet stretches, active stretches, or uneven bursts. It is not a profit chart.
  • Action Frequency: how often the behaviour created actions during the study window. This helps you know whether it is usually quiet, occasional, or busy.
  • Reaction Style: how quickly or patiently the behaviour tended to respond after its conditions lined up.
  • Move Personality: the kind of movement the behaviour tended to participate in, such as calmer, balanced, bold, or aggressive movement.
  • Noise Tolerance: how much messy or unclear movement the behaviour historically tolerated before stepping aside. Lower noise tolerance usually means it waits for cleaner conditions. Higher noise tolerance means it can remain active through messier movement.
  • Longest Quiet Period: the longest stretch where the behaviour created no action. Silence can be normal when the behaviour’s rules are not aligned.
  • Allowed Window Adherence: how often the behaviour stayed inside the time or window rules used in the review. This is about discipline and boundaries.
  • Typical Scenarios: examples of situations where the behaviour historically acted or stepped aside. These should help you understand context, not give advice.
  • Behaviour Timeline: a period-by-period view of when activity was higher or lower. It gives more detail than the Activity Visual and helps show whether activity was spread out or clustered.
  • Important Disclaimers: short boundaries to keep in mind when reading study-history information.

How to compare behaviours calmly

  • Use descriptions and study-history labels to understand fit with your comfort level.
  • Pay attention to account pressure, quiet periods, movement sensitivity, and market differences.
  • Confirm connected account scope and compatible markets before activation.
  • Use Flow after activation to review the actions Chilla records for your instruction.
