Work Capacity & Guardrails

Chilla is designed to work within explicit authorization, product safeguards, account-provider rules, and the capacity available to your tier.

Guardrails exist to constrain Chilla’s work, protect service integrity, and keep authorized financial activity inside the product boundaries that apply to it.

1. Why guardrails exist

Chilla works in a high-stakes environment. Product controls are designed to prevent unauthorized scope changes, constrain activity when required conditions are not met, protect service stability, and keep account/provider state aligned with the work Chilla is allowed to do.

Guardrails can block, pause, or limit activity. They do not promise a profitable outcome or remove market risk.

2. Work Capacity

Work Capacity represents the service capacity available for Chilla to keep working during the applicable usage window. The user-facing product shows available capacity as a percentage, such as 100%, 64%, or 0%.

Raw internal capacity counters and operation costs are not part of the public product experience. Capacity is a service-resource control, not a financial result, performance score, speed promise, or market signal.

As Chilla works, available capacity may decrease. The percentage resets according to the tier and reset window shown in the product.

3. Capacity tiers

Your subscription tier determines the amount of Work Capacity available and may also determine how many live accounts Chilla can work on.

  • Chill Architect: Limited capacity.
  • Chill Mode: Standard capacity.
  • Deep Chill: Expanded capacity.
  • Peak Chill: Highest capacity.

Current pricing and live-account allowances are listed on Pricing.

If available capacity reaches 0%, work that depends on that capacity may pause until the applicable reset or another product action restores availability. Reaching a capacity limit does not rewrite the material configuration you already authorized.

4. Authorization is separate from intelligence

Chilla may understand a goal, explain available product options, or prepare bounded work for review. None of those things gives it authority to begin or materially change live financial work by itself.

Where approval is required, the exact material configuration must be explicitly authorized before it becomes active. A later material change goes through a new validated revision and approval before replacing the active configuration.

Intelligence is not authority. Product safeguards are built so a statement in conversation cannot substitute for the authorization the system requires.

5. Comfort and other safeguards

Comfort and related safeguards are enforced by Beaverly’s canonical financial systems rather than reconstructed from conversational text. Depending on the task, account, market, provider, and product state, safeguards may include validated sizing, account/provider minimums, authorized boundaries, eligibility checks, stale-condition checks, or other controls.

A safeguard can cause Chilla to wait, skip, pause, or refuse an action when required conditions are not met. These controls reduce avoidable product risk but cannot guarantee a specific loss limit in every market or provider condition.

6. Flow remains the factual record

Flow is the product record of what Chilla did, did not do, and why. It can include entries, exits, passed actions, warnings, and live monitoring events produced by the underlying financial systems.

Conversation may summarize or explain Flow, but the factual Flow record remains the source for activity reporting. Chilla should not invent financial activity that is absent from canonical account or Flow data.

7. Temporary service protection

Beaverly may temporarily restrict, pause, or refuse some activity when needed to protect users or the Services, including where:

  • capacity or service limits are reached;
  • account-provider state is unavailable or inconsistent;
  • market or system data is stale, incomplete, or unsafe to act on;
  • security, abuse, fraud, or integrity concerns are detected;
  • technical recovery or reconciliation is required;
  • legal or regulatory obligations require a restriction.

Where the product can determine a safe, truthful state, it should prefer waiting, reconciling, or requiring explicit continuation rather than improvising.

8. What capacity and guardrails do not mean

  • Capacity does not estimate profit, quality, or expected market activity.
  • A blocked or skipped action does not prove that the action would have made or lost money.
  • A safeguard does not guarantee that loss cannot occur.
  • More capacity does not mean better financial outcomes.
  • A higher tier does not change the non-advisory, non-custodial nature of the Services.

Questions about capacity or product limits can be sent to support@beaverlyai.com.
